Understanding Citation Search in the Summa Theologiae

The Summa Theologiae (ST) by St. Thomas Aquinas is structured in a strict hierarchical order. The Citation Search engine allows users to navigate directly to precise sections of the text—down to specific structural elements like thesis statements or individual objections—using standard theological abbreviations and flexible formatting styles.

1. Understanding the Four Volumes

The Summa is partitioned into four primary volume sections. The search system maps structural Latin abbreviations as well as descriptive thematic titles to identify each volume:

Volume Code Latin / Structural Title Thematic Scope Description Accepted Variants
Volume I First Part (FP) God, Creation, and Divine Government I, FP, 1
Volume I-II First Part of the Second Part (FS) Human Action, Virtues, and the Moral Law I-II, FS, 1-2, I_II
Volume II-II Second Part of the Second Part (SS) The Particular Virtues and Vices II-II, SS, 2-2, II_II
Volume III Third Part (TP) Christ, the Sacraments, and Eschatology III, TP, 3
Structural vs. Thematic System Mapping

The search engine supports two mapping dictionaries internally:

  • Structural Designations: Uses traditional scholastic nomenclature such as FP (Prima Pars), FS (Prima Secundae), SS (Secunda Secundae), and TP (Tertia Pars).
  • Thematic Descriptions: Connects volume indexes directly to their theological subject matter (e.g., mapping Volume I-II to Human Action, Virtues, and the Moral Law).

2. Anatomy of a Citation Search Query

A citation query consists of up to four hierarchical components:

[ST Prefix + Volume], [Question], [Article], [Target Section]
  • ST Prefix & Volume: Mandatory anchor (e.g., ST I or ST FP).
  • Question (Q): Specifies the overarching question topic (e.g., Q. 2 or q. 90).
  • Article (Art): Specifies the sub-inquiry under discussion (e.g., Art. 3 or a. 1).
  • Target Section (Optional): Drills down to specific structural elements inside an article:
    • Objections: obj. 2 (Counter-arguments being addressed)
    • Authority (Sed Contra): sc, s.c., authority ("On the contrary...")
    • Thesis (Respondeo): resp, body, c., thesis ("I answer that...")
    • Rebuttals: ad 1, reply 1 (Replies to specific objections)

3. Representative Search Query Examples

The parser accepts multiple formatting styles, ranging from formal longhand notation to shorthand dot-notation.

Level 1: Question-Level Queries

Retrieve all articles under a specific Question.

ST I, Q. 2  |  ST FP, q. 2  |  ST I, 2
➔ Target: Volume I (First Part), Question 2 (The Existence of God).

Level 2: Article-Level Queries

Navigate directly to a specific Article within a Question.

ST I, Q. 2, Art. 3  |  ST FP, q. 2, a. 3  |  ST I.2.3
➔ Target: Volume I, Question 2, Article 3 ("Whether God exists?" - The Five Ways).

Level 3: Section-Targeted Queries

Target specific structural components inside an Article.

A. Targeting the Thesis / Body (Respondeo)

ST I, Q. 2, Art. 3, resp.  |  ST FP, q. 2, a. 3, body  |  ST I, 2, 3, c.  |  ST I, Q. 90, Art. 2, thesis
➔ Target: Focuses directly on Aquinas's main affirmative position (Respondeo / Body / Corpus).

B. Targeting Objections

ST I-II, Q. 90, Art. 1, obj. 2  |  ST FS, q. 90, a. 1, obj. 2  |  ST I-II, 90, 1, obj. 2
➔ Target: Focuses specifically on Objection #2 regarding the nature of Law in Volume I-II.

C. Targeting Rebuttals (Replies to Objections)

ST II-II, Q. 23, Art. 1, ad 1  |  ST SS, q. 23, a. 1, ad 1  |  ST II-II, 23, 1, ad 1
➔ Target: Focuses on Reply to Objection 1 concerning Charity in Volume II-II.

D. Targeting Authority (On the Contrary / Sed Contra)

ST I, Q. 2, Art. 3, sc  |  ST FP, q. 2, a. 3, s.c.  |  ST I, 2, 3, sc  |  ST I, Q. 90, Art. 2, authority
➔ Target: Focuses on the counter-authority quotation (Sed Contra) preceding the main argument.

4. Search Engine Features & Flexibility

  • Case Insensitive: Queries like st fp, q. 2, a. 3 and ST FP, Q. 2, A. 3 yield identical results.
  • Punctuation Invariant: You can use spaces, commas, or dots (e.g., ST I.2.3 vs. ST I, 2, 3).
  • Prefix Optionality: Including ST is recommended, but short-code designations like FP 2.3 or FS 90.1 are automatically normalized.
